# Ideal Customer Profile: COMMARKAI

> Draft generated 2026-08-29 | Based on: COMMARKAI, a founder-led AI
> automation and business-systems company built on three pillars,
> communications, marketing, and AI. Sells custom websites and apps,
> marketing and workflow automation, lead systems, and AI
> consulting/training to non-technical owners of service businesses.

## ICP Summary

COMMARKAI's best-fit customer today is an owner-operated service business
doing roughly $500K to $10M a year, with 5 to 75 staff, in a trade or
personal-services vertical where the owner is not technical and does not
want to be. They are busy enough that manual follow up, a weak website,
and scattered tools are actively costing them work, and they have enough
revenue to spend $2,500 on a build plus $250 a month without a committee.
They buy from a person they trust, not from a pitch, and they value
someone who will set it up, document it, and not leave them locked out.

The strongest signals are a founder who is the bottleneck, a website that
looks a decade old or does not convert, a recent jump in demand (new
location, new hire, seasonal peak), and tools that do not talk to each
other. The wrong-fit customer is a pre-revenue startup, a large
enterprise with procurement and security review, a business that wants
the cheapest option, or one already deep inside a competitor's platform
with no reason to move.

A secondary, later-stage ICP exists: mid-market and enterprise teams on
Azure, Oracle, Google Cloud, or AWS who need governed agentic workflows.
That is a different sale (longer cycle, security review, higher price)
and is not the focus while COMMARKAI is a solo operation. It is listed
below as a secondary vertical, not a primary target.

## Technographic Profile

- **Current stack (good fit):** a DIY website builder they have outgrown
  (Wix, Squarespace, GoDaddy), a general inbox, a spreadsheet or
  notebook for leads, maybe one point tool (a booking app, a basic email
  tool). QuickBooks or similar for accounting. Active on Instagram,
  Facebook, or LinkedIn but not converting it.
- **Technical sophistication:** 1 to 2 on a 5 scale. They can use apps,
  they cannot configure integrations, and they do not want to learn.
- **Integration needs:** contact form to CRM to email, calendar booking,
  payment link, review request. Nothing exotic.
- **Adoption pattern:** fast follower, not early adopter. They move when
  a peer or a trusted advisor tells them it works.
- **Digital maturity tells:** old copyright year in the site footer, no
  online booking, no reviews shown on site, slow mobile load, a
  Linktree standing in for a real site.
- **Too sophisticated to be a fit:** already running HubSpot
  Professional, a full Salesforce org, or an in-house developer.

## Behavioral Signals

- **Events:** regional trade shows, franchise conventions, chamber of

- **Communities:** trade-specific Facebook groups, subreddits for their

## Budget Qualifiers

- **Revenue floor for a viable deal:** about $500K a year. Below that,
  the $250/mo is usually the sticking point.
- **Deal size sweet spot:** $2,500 to $6,000 for the build, plus $250 to
  $750 a month depending on scope. Value-based pricing at 10 to 30
  percent of the value created applies above the base for bigger outcomes.
- **Minimum viable deal:** the $2,500 build floor. Never below without
  Ketsia's explicit yes (rulebook section 3).
- **Tech spend today:** most fit clients spend a few hundred dollars a
  month across scattered subscriptions and do not track it. Part of the
  pitch is consolidating that.
- **Budget cycle:** owner-run, so budget is "now" when the pain is
  acute. Tie outreach to trigger events, not to a fiscal calendar.
- **Price sensitivity:** they will say "that's a lot" once. If the
  outcome is clear they pay it. If they push hard on price, they are
  usually a wrong fit.
- **ROI expectation:** one or two extra jobs a month should cover the
  retainer. Say that plainly.
- **Budget-authority tell from outside:** they are hiring, they have a
  second location, they run paid ads, they have a real payroll.

## Channel Strategy

- **Where they research:** Google, referrals from other owners, Facebook
  groups, Instagram for beauty and creative, the occasional trade
  directory. Not G2 or Capterra.
- **Best contact methods, ranked:** 1. warm referral or introduction,
  2. Instagram or LinkedIn DM for verticals where the owner is active,
  3. a specific, personal cold email tied to a trigger, 4. local
  in-person events. Cold phone last.
- **Decision process:** owner initiates, owner evaluates, owner approves.
  One to three touches, then a call, then a decision inside a week for
  small builds.
- **Content that works:** short before-and-after case examples framed
  generically (no client identity, rulebook section 4), a simple "what
  a lead system looks like" walkthrough, a one-page proposal, a live
  demo of the booking flow.
- **Cadence:** 3 to 5 touches over 2 weeks for cold, spaced 3 to 4 days
  apart, each adding one concrete thing, not just "checking in".
- **Trust signals:** the quality of COMMARKAI's own site and brand,
  clear plain-language explanations, a documented handoff so they are
  never locked out, and a real Toronto business address.

## Negative ICP (who to avoid)

1. **Pre-revenue or idea-stage.** No budget, scope creep, wants a
   co-founder not a vendor.
2. **Large enterprise with procurement.** Security questionnaires,
   legal review, 6 to 12 month cycles a solo operation cannot carry.
   (Exception: the secondary agentic-workflow ICP, handled deliberately
   and priced for it.)
3. **Price-first buyers.** If the first and last question is "how
   cheap", the relationship will be painful and churn fast.
4. **Already on a full platform.** Running HubSpot Professional,
   Salesforce, or a similar committed stack with an admin. Switching
   cost is high and the need is low.
5. **Wants ongoing hands-on-keyboard staffing.** COMMARKAI builds and
   maintains systems, it is not a staffing agency or a virtual
   assistant service.
6. **Highly regulated with no compliance budget.** Clinics, legal,
   financial advice businesses that need heavy compliance work but will
   not pay for it.
7. **No single decision maker.** Partnerships where two or three people
   must all agree and none will own it.
8. **Distressed or non-paying.** Cash flow problems, asking for equity
   or "exposure", late on other vendors.
9. **Outside North America or a big timezone gap.** Support and
   turnaround suffer for a solo operation.
10. **"Just exploring."** No trigger, no timeline, no budget. Add to
    nurture, spend no sales time.

## ICP Scoring Rubric

100 points. Score a lead in under 5 minutes.

| Category | Max | What it measures |
|---|---|---|
| Firmographic fit | 25 | Revenue, employees, vertical, geography, stage |
| Technographic fit | 15 | Outgrown a DIY tool, low sophistication, simple integration needs |
| Pain point alignment | 20 | Severity and urgency of the leads/website/bottleneck pain |
| Budget capacity | 20 | Revenue over $500K, real payroll, hiring, paid ads, second location |
| Contact access | 10 | Owner identified, warm path or active DM channel |
| Timing signals | 10 | A live trigger event: new location, hire, rebrand, seasonal peak |

**Category guide (firmographic fit, 25):**
- 25: primary vertical, inside revenue and headcount range, North
  America, 2+ years operating, clear growth signal
- 19: 4 of 5 criteria, one minor gap
- 13: 3 of 5, needs validation
- 6: 1 to 2 criteria, marginal
- 0: wrong vertical and wrong size, disqualified on firmographics alone

Apply the same 0 / 25 / 50 / 75 / 100 percent logic to the other five
categories.

**Grade bands:**
- **A (85 to 100):** pursue now, personal multi-touch outreach within 24
  hours
- **B (70 to 84):** high priority, personalized outreach this week
- **C (50 to 69):** worth a light touch, semi-personal, watch for trigger
  changes
- **D (below 50):** do not pursue, nurture list at most

**60-second checklist:**
1. Primary or secondary target vertical? (Y/N)
2. Revenue likely $500K or more? (Y/N)
3. A live trigger event? (Y/N)
4. Can you name their top pain? (Y/N)
5. Can you reach the owner directly? (Y/N)

5 yes = likely A. 3 to 4 = likely B. 1 to 2 = likely C. 0 = D.

## Buyer Personas

### Persona 1: The Owner Who Is the Bottleneck

- **Profile:** Founder-operator, 35 to 55, runs a construction, HVAC, or
  home-services company with 8 to 40 staff. Learned the trade, then
  learned the business. Not technical.
- **Day:** on site or on the phone from 7am, quoting in the evening,
  doing the books on Sunday. Misses calls constantly.
- **Goals and KPIs:** jobs booked, crews utilized, cash flow steady,
  fewer nights working.
- **Pains, in their words:** "I'm losing jobs because I can't call
  people back fast enough." "My website's embarrassing." "I can't take a
  week off without everything stopping."
- **Info diet:** trade YouTube, contractor Facebook groups, other owners
  at the supply house.
- **Objections:** "I don't have time to learn another system." "I've
  been burned by a marketing guy before." "Can you just do it for me?"
- **Messaging that lands:** "You keep running the jobs. We set up the
  system that catches the leads and books them, and we document it so
  it's yours." Subject line: "the calls you're missing this week".
- **Turn-offs:** jargon, dashboards they have to check, anything that
  adds a task to their day.
- **How to win them:** show the booking flow live, promise the handoff
  doc, give one referral name.

### Persona 2: The Beauty or Wellness Founder Scaling Up

- **Profile:** Owner of a salon, clinic, studio, or product line, 28 to
  45, strong on Instagram, weak on systems. Often opening a second
  location or launching online.
- **Day:** with clients, managing staff, posting content, handling DMs
  that are really booking requests.
- **Goals and KPIs:** chairs or slots filled, retail or online sales,
  brand growth, a team that runs without her.
- **Pains, in their words:** "My DMs are a mess and I know I'm losing
  bookings." "The website doesn't match the brand." "I want to sell
  online but I don't know where to start."
- **Info diet:** Instagram, beauty and wellness expos, founder podcasts.
- **Objections:** "It needs to look right, not templated." "Will it work
  with my booking app?" "I don't want it to feel corporate."
- **Messaging that lands:** cool, not corporate, female-friendly, "a
  site and a booking flow that look like your brand and actually
  convert the DMs". The COMMARKAI brand itself is the proof.
- **Turn-offs:** ugly templates, stiff tone, anything that reads male or
  corporate.
- **How to win them:** show brand-matched design work, a clean mobile
  booking demo, the Instagram-to-booking path.

### Persona 3: The Agency or Studio Lead Who Needs Leverage

- **Profile:** Runs a small creative agency, production studio, or
  consultancy, 30 to 50, 4 to 20 people. Technical enough to know what
  they want, too busy to build it.
- **Day:** client work, pitches, managing a small team, firefighting
  ops.
- **Goals and KPIs:** billable utilization, pipeline, margin, less admin
  drag.
- **Pains, in their words:** "Our own marketing is the cobbler's
  kids." "Intake and onboarding eat a day a week." "We keep
  re-inventing the same proposal."
- **Info diet:** LinkedIn, agency owner communities, Slack groups.
- **Objections:** "We could build this ourselves if we had time." "Is
  this just Zapier with a markup?" "What happens when you're not
  around?"
- **Messaging that lands:** "You bill the client work. We build your
  intake, onboarding, and proposal system so it stops eating your
  week." Peer-to-peer tone, specifics, no fluff.
- **Turn-offs:** overclaiming, vague "AI transformation" talk, no clear
  handoff.
- **How to win them:** a tight scoped build, the documented handoff, a
  fixed price.

## Prospecting Playbook

- **Where to find them:**
  - Google Maps and local search for a trade plus city, then check the
    website age and whether they have online booking
  - Instagram location and hashtag search for beauty and wellness in
    target cities
  - LinkedIn search: owner or founder titles in target industries,
    filtered to company size 2 to 50, Canada and US
  - Facebook trade groups and local business groups
  - Job boards: businesses posting for an office manager, receptionist,
    or marketing coordinator in target verticals
  - Referrals from every delivered project, asked for explicitly
- **Search strings to start with:**
  - `site:instagram.com "book now" [city] salon`
  - LinkedIn: `("owner" OR "founder") AND ("HVAC" OR "plumbing" OR
    "electrical") ` company headcount 2 to 50, geography Canada/US
  - Google: `[trade] [city]` then sort by sites with a copyright year
    two or more years old
- **Signals to monitor:** new location announcements, "now hiring"
  posts, rebrands, funding or expansion news for the larger secondary
  accounts, a competitor in their town launching a slick new site.
- **Prioritization when you have 100 matches:** rank by (1) live trigger
  event, (2) revenue signal (payroll, ads, second location), (3) how
  bad the current website is, (4) whether you have a warm path.
- **Enrichment checklist before outreach:**
  1. Owner name and role
  2. Company size estimate
  3. Current website platform and last-updated signal
  4. Whether they have online booking
  5. Whether reviews are shown on the site
  6. Instagram or LinkedIn activity level of the owner
  7. Any recent trigger event
  8. Current point tools visible (booking app, chat widget)
  9. Whether they run paid ads
  10. A specific, true observation to open the message with
- **Warm-path tactics:** engage with the owner's posts for a week before
  reaching out, get introduced through a past client in the same town,
  meet them at a local event.
- **Timing:** for trades, reach out ahead of their busy season, not
  during it. For beauty and retail, before Q4. Mid-week mornings.
- **3-check disqualification speed check:** wrong vertical, under ~$500K
  revenue with no growth signal, already on a full CRM platform. Any one
  of these, stop.

## Competitive Context

- **Primary alternatives the prospect considers:**
  - Doing nothing (the real competitor). Trigger to beat: the cost of
    missed leads.
  - A cheap freelancer on a marketplace. COMMARKAI's edge: a connected
    system plus a documented handoff, not a one-off deliverable.
  - A DIY builder plus a pile of apps. Edge: it works together and
    someone else set it up.
  - A local marketing agency on a big retainer. Edge: scoped, priced,
    and owned by the client, not a monthly dependency with no artifact.
- **Positioning statement:** For non-technical owners of growing service
  businesses, COMMARKAI builds the website and lead system that catches
  and books the work, sets it up, and hands it over documented, for a
  fixed build price plus a small monthly, not an open-ended agency
  retainer.
- **Common displacement:** from a stale DIY site and manual follow up,
  triggered by a demand spike the owner cannot keep up with.
- **Market trends in COMMARKAI's favor:** small businesses expect
  online booking now, buyers judge a business by its site in seconds,
  and owners are more open than ever to "automation" as a benefit as
  long as it is framed around their own time and growth, not the
  mechanism (rulebook section 2).

## ICP Maintenance Guide

- **Review cadence:** quarterly, or after any pricing change, a new
  service line, or a run of unexpected wins or losses.
- **Update triggers:**
  - 3+ closed deals outside these parameters
  - 3+ losses to the same alternative or objection
  - A new core service (for example a productized offer)
  - A pricing model change
  - The full-time transition changing capacity and target deal size
- **Feedback loop:** after running `sales-prospect` on 10 or more real
  companies, compare the scores to what actually closed and adjust the
  weights.
- **Version control:** date-stamp this file on every change and keep the
  prior version in git history.
